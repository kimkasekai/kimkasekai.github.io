How to Create Professional ID Photos at Home With Free Tools

Published: August 5, 2026

By kimkasekai, developer of miniPhotoShop

← Back to Blog

Passport offices and photo studios charge a surprising amount for something you can produce at home in fifteen minutes—if you know the rules. This guide walks through the entire process: shooting, sizing, background cleanup, and printing a sheet of ID photos that passes inspection.

Know Your Dimensions First

Different documents demand different sizes, so check the requirement before you shoot:

  • 1-inch photo (25 × 35 mm): Common for resumes, employee badges, and many application forms.
  • 2-inch photo (35 × 45 mm or 51 × 51 mm): The standard for passports and visas in most countries.
  • Head position: The head (from chin to crown) should occupy roughly 70–80% of the frame height, with eyes in the upper third.
  • Resolution: Aim for at least 300 DPI at final size—a 2-inch photo needs roughly 413 × 531 pixels minimum.

Set Up Your Lighting

Lighting separates amateur snapshots from acceptable ID photos:

  • Face a window. Soft daylight from the front is free and flattering. Avoid direct sun, which causes squinting and harsh shadows.
  • No side lighting. Light coming from one side creates shadow across half the face—an automatic rejection in many offices.
  • Kill mixed lighting. Don't combine window light with ceiling lamps; the color casts are hard to fix.
  • Watch for glasses glare. Tilt your head down slightly or adjust angles until your eyes are clearly visible through the lenses.

Nail the Background

Most official photos require a plain white or light gray background. Stand 1–2 meters in front of a smooth white wall to avoid casting shadows on it. If a perfect wall isn't available, don't panic: shoot against any uniform surface, then use an online editor like miniPhotoShop to remove the background and drop in clean white. The cutout approach also lets you fix slightly off-white walls that would otherwise fail inspection.

Crop With Precision

  1. Open your best shot in the editor and select the crop tool.
  2. Enter exact pixel dimensions for your target size (for example, 413 × 531 px for a 35 × 45 mm photo at 300 DPI).
  3. Position the crop so eyes sit about one-third from the top and equal space appears on both sides of the face.
  4. Keep shoulders visible unless the specification says otherwise.
  5. Straighten the horizon—a tilted head is grounds for rejection.

Build a Print Layout

One photo per sheet wastes paper and money. Instead, arrange multiple copies on standard 4×6 inch or A4 photo paper:

  • Create a new canvas sized to your paper (1200 × 1800 px for 4×6 at 300 DPI).
  • Copy your cropped ID photo and paste it repeatedly in a grid—typically eight 2-inch photos fit on one 4×6 sheet.
  • Leave a small gap between copies so scissors have room to work.
  • Export as high-quality JPG and print at a pharmacy kiosk or home printer on glossy photo paper.

Final Checklist

  • Neutral expression, mouth closed, both ears or edges of face visible where required
  • No hats, headbands, or heavy filters (religious headwear is generally permitted)
  • Recent photo—most offices want it taken within the last six months
  • Sharp focus with no red-eye or shadows

Follow these steps and your homemade ID photos will be indistinguishable from studio work—at a fraction of the price.
