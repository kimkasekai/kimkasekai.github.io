Photo Editing Tips Every E-Commerce Seller Needs to Know

Published: June 29, 2026

← Back to Blog

Shoppers cannot touch your products, so your photographs do the selling. Studies consistently show that listing images influence purchase decisions more than reviews or price comparisons. The good news: you do not need a studio budget to produce catalog-quality visuals, just disciplined editing habits.

Clean Up the Background First

Marketplace standards demand a pure white backdrop for main images. If your raw shots carry gray tints or distracting clutter, remove the background entirely and place the product on a fresh white canvas. Keep a transparent PNG master so you can drop the same cutout onto lifestyle scenes later without redoing the work.

Standardize Size and Alignment

A grid of mismatched thumbnails reads as chaos. Pick one square dimension, commonly 2000 by 2000 pixels, and crop every product to it. Center the item, leave equal margins on all sides, and rotate so verticals are truly vertical. Consistency across your catalog signals professionalism at a glance.

Fix Brightness and Contrast

Camera phones underexpose dark products and blow out shiny ones. Nudge brightness until whites read as white, then add gentle contrast so edges pop against the background. Watch colors carefully: a navy shirt that edits into black will generate returns. Calibrate your screen and compare the on-screen product against the physical item before publishing.

Add Natural Shadows

Floating objects look fake. Two shadow styles cover most needs:

  • Contact shadow: A tight, dark blur directly beneath the base of the product, suggesting it rests on a surface.
  • Drop shadow: A softer offset silhouette behind the object, adding depth on colored backgrounds.

Keep opacity between 15% and 35%; heavy black shadows scream amateur hour.

Build Lifestyle Mockups

After mastering cutouts, composite your product into real environments: a mug on a kitchen counter, a lamp in a cozy bedroom. Match perspective, lighting direction, and color temperature between the product layer and the scene, then unify everything with a shared adjustment layer so the composite feels photographed rather than pasted.

Optimize Files for the Web

  • Export JPEGs at 70–85% quality; visually identical to maximum quality at a fraction of the size.
  • Keep main images near 200 KB so pages load fast on mobile connections.
  • Use PNG only when transparency is required, since it produces larger files.
  • Fast-loading pages rank better in search and convert better with impatient shoppers.

Apply these six habits to every listing and your storefront will instantly stand apart from competitors still uploading raw phone snapshots.
