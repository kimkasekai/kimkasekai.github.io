Choose Your Collage Style First

Two main approaches exist, and picking the right one saves hours of frustration:

  • Grid collages: Photos snap into predefined cells of equal or varied size. Perfect for uniform sets like product shots, family portraits, or event recaps. Grids look tidy with almost zero effort.
  • Freeform collages: You place, rotate, and overlap images anywhere on the canvas. Ideal for scrapbook aesthetics, mood boards, and artistic compositions where deliberate messiness is the point.

If you are unsure, start with a grid. It is nearly impossible to make one look bad.

Select Photos With a Common Thread

The fastest way to ruin a collage is mixing unrelated images. Before uploading, pick photos sharing at least one element: same event, similar lighting, matching color palette, or consistent subject matter. Five to nine images usually works best â€” fewer feels sparse, more becomes visual noise.

Nail the Layout

  • Lead with your strongest image. Give it the largest cell or the top-left position, since Western eyes scan from there first.
  • Mix orientations. Pair landscape shots with portrait shots so no cell forces awkward cropping.
  • Balance busy and calm. Alternate detailed close-ups with simpler wide shots to give viewers' eyes resting places.
  • Keep faces away from edges. Crop so heads sit comfortably inside their cells, not sliced by borders.

Photos taken minutes apart can have wildly different colors. Apply the same filter or adjustment preset to every image so the set reads as one piece.
