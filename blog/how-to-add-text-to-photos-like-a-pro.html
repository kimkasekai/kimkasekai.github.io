How to Add Text to Photos Like a Professional Designer

Published: August 1, 2026

By kimkasekai, developer of miniPhotoShop

← Back to Blog

A well-placed quote or headline can turn an ordinary photo into a shareable poster. A badly placed one makes the whole image look amateurish. The difference comes down to a handful of techniques that anyone can learn in minutes.

Pick Fonts With Intent

Stick to two fonts maximum per design. Pair a bold display font for the main message with a clean sans-serif for supporting details. Script and handwritten fonts work for short emotional phrases but become unreadable at small sizes. If your photo is busy, choose a heavier typeface so the words do not drown in the background detail.

Size for Hierarchy

Your most important word should be the largest element on the canvas. A common poster formula: make the headline roughly three times taller than the subtitle. Keep body text above 16 pixels if the graphic will be viewed on phones. Step back from the screen and squint; if any line disappears, it is too small.

Guarantee Contrast

White text on a bright sky is invisible, and dark text on shadows vanishes too. Three reliable fixes:

  • Darken the area behind text with a semi-transparent black rectangle or gradient overlay
  • Add a drop shadow to push letters off the background
  • Use an outline (stroke) in a contrasting color around each letter

The shadow-plus-outline combination is what most movie posters use, because it keeps text readable over almost any imagery.

Align Deliberately

Amateurs center everything by default. Professionals pick one alignment and commit: left-aligned blocks feel modern and editorial, centered layouts feel formal and symmetrical. Give text breathing room by keeping it away from the edges of the frame; a margin of about 5% of the image width on all sides instantly looks more polished.

Experiment With Blending Modes

Once your text layer exists, try changing its blend mode. Overlay lets the photo texture show through the letters for a gritty, integrated look. Soft Light creates a subtle tone-on-tone effect perfect for elegant designs. Reducing opacity to 70-85% helps text feel like part of the photograph instead of something pasted on top.

A Quick Poster Walkthrough

  1. Open your photo and add a dark gradient across the bottom third
  2. Type your headline in a bold condensed font, left-aligned near the lower edge
  3. Add a smaller subtitle underneath in a neutral sans-serif
  4. Apply a soft shadow to both layers
  5. Export as PNG for crisp lettering

You can build all of this in miniPhotoShop without installing anything. Open a photo, grab the text tool, and practice these five principles; within a few attempts your overlays will look like they came from a design studio.
